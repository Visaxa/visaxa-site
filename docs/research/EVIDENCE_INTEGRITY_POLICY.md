# Visaxa Research Evidence Integrity Policy

Status: **CANONICAL EDITORIAL POLICY**

Owner: **Visaxa Research editorial maintainer**

Applies to: all Research designs, drafts, evidence packs, publication candidates and published articles.

## Core rule

Never imply that Visaxa performed a product test, observed a vendor behavior, experienced a failure or verified a workflow unless that testing actually occurred and retained evidence supports the statement.

## Evidence boundaries

1. Named vendors are allowed as neutral examples and primary evidence sources.
2. Truthful comparative analysis is allowed when the compared claim, scope and evidence are explicit.
3. Official documentation supports what the vendor currently documents within the cited product, plan, region and conditions. It does not by itself prove error-free operation, universal availability or a successful customer outcome.
4. Missing documentation means **UNKNOWN**. It does not prove that a capability is absent.
5. User reviews and community reports remain attributed to the users or discussions reviewed. They may identify hypotheses; they do not automatically establish product facts.
6. Hypothetical scenarios must be unmistakably hypothetical. They may illustrate a decision or test, but must not read as Visaxa first-party observation.
7. A first-party test claim requires retained evidence describing the product and version where material, account/plan/region, configuration, role, device, scenario, expected result, observed result and test date.
8. Analysis and inference must be distinguishable from the source fact that supports them. Prefer the pattern: **documented fact → bounded interpretation → salon-owner question or test**.
9. Named competitors are sources and examples, not defendants. Do not add adversarial framing, product superiority language or Visaxa promotion to Research analysis.
10. Product claims are time-sensitive. Record source and access dates where material, refresh volatile claims, and narrow or remove a claim when its current source no longer supports it.

## Internal claim taxonomy

- **A — Direct official evidence:** current first-party vendor documentation directly supports the scoped statement.
- **B — Independent authoritative evidence:** a reliable external authority directly supports the statement.
- **C — User report:** an attributed user or community report describes an experience.
- **D — Visaxa first-party test:** Visaxa conducted the test and retained sufficient evidence.
- **E — Analysis / inference:** a conclusion drawn from identified evidence.
- **F — Hypothetical scenario:** an illustrative situation that was not observed.
- **G — Unsupported:** no adequate support exists.

Public prose does not need these labels. Editorial review does.

## Required treatment

- A and B may remain factual with accurate scope, date and citation.
- C must remain explicitly attributed and must not be generalized into a product fact.
- D may describe an actual test only to the scope supported by the retained record.
- E must be phrased as analysis rather than as a source statement.
- F must signal its hypothetical status naturally without turning the article into compliance prose.
- G must be narrowed to a supported claim or removed. Never invent a source to preserve it.

One concise qualification is normally enough. Precision should come from sentence construction, not repetitive disclaimers.
