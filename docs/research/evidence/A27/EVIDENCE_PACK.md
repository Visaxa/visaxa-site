## Evidence boundary

This pack supports a cautious method for deciding D24. It supports observing work in context, separating observations from interpretations, examining task, individual, and organizational conditions, and treating workarounds as potentially mixed in purpose, benefit, and risk. It does not establish how common any workaround is in salons, diagnose a salon, prove a named product limitation, or show that a particular remedy will work.

The four-job classification used by A27—knowledge, friction, burden/incentive, and control—is an editorial synthesis of the canonical graph and the evidence below. It is not a validated psychometric or operational instrument.

## Evidence required for a real salon diagnosis

1. E02 record of the real task: role, trigger, device, official route, workaround route, timing, handoffs, duplicate entry, exceptions, and downstream consequence.
2. E03 interview record: purpose in the staff member’s words, relevant knowledge, perceived risk, access, responsibility, incentives, and prior practice or training.
3. E10 artifact and system evidence: affected client/appointment/payment record, available audit or access events, record freshness, permissions, and unlogged gaps.
4. A privacy-safe record that excludes unnecessary client or employee personal data and retains consent where observation or recording requires it.
5. Repeated evidence across material roles or exceptions before generalizing beyond one occurrence.

## Wording constraints

Use “can,” “may,” “in the studied settings,” “the record shows,” and “remains unknown” where evidence is bounded. Call the four categories a practical classification or decision aid.

Avoid “employees resist change,” “research proves,” “all salons,” “the system cannot,” “training fixes,” “unsafe” without a named control/consequence, and any implication that Visaxa observed the hypothetical salon.

## Final evidence status

**READY WITH KNOWN LIMITATIONS**

The pack supports publication of a cautious workaround-classification method. It does not support a salon diagnosis, prevalence estimate, intervention result, named-vendor finding, or product claim.
