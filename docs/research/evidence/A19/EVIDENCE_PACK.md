## Evidence boundary

This pack supports a method for comparing the economic change when a salon crosses a staffing, location, usage, workflow, or exit threshold. Official vendor sources establish only the prices, limits, inclusions, and claims displayed for their stated market and date. They do not establish what a salon will buy, use, save, earn, or avoid.

## Pricing patterns established without ranking vendors

The official pages demonstrate that “growth” does not have one price unit:

- a location can trigger another recurring charge;
- a user, bookable professional, or calendar can trigger another charge;
- a team-size band can trigger a different plan;
- messages, marketing, phone, payroll, reporting, storage, or integrations can become usage fees or add-ons;
- payment method and volume can change processing expense;
- migration and implementation assistance may be included, paid, custom, or insufficiently specified;
- legacy and custom pricing can differ from public prices.

This supports the A19 threshold method. It does not support a universal cost ranking because candidates meter different objects and the salon's operating state determines exposure.

## Net-economic-cost definitions

| Term | Safe definition | Evidence needed before treating it as money |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time saved | Measured reduction in task minutes for the same completed outcome | Repeated task observation under comparable conditions |
| Payroll saving | Actual reduction in paid hours or total employer labor cost | Payroll/timecard records and unchanged required outcome |
| Avoided hire | A documented role that would otherwise have been added at a defined threshold but was not needed after the workflow change | Dated staffing plan, demand trigger, task coverage, service level, and post-change capacity evidence |
| Recovered revenue | Completed paid sales that would not otherwise have occurred | Event-level baseline/counterfactual and payment record |
| Incremental contribution profit | Incremental revenue minus commissions, labor directly tied to service, materials, processing, and other variable costs | Service-level price and variable-cost records |
| Reduced loss/correction cost | A measurable payment, refund, rework, or error cost that no longer occurs | Before/after event records with comparable exposure |
| Net economic outcome | Realized benefits minus incremental recurring, usage, implementation, transition, transferred-work, and exit costs | All relevant records plus explicit time horizon |

## Muse pilot assessment

### What the pilot can support

- owners may describe the problem as running without a receptionist, handling calls after hours, or reducing no-shows rather than as ROI;
- the “avoided receptionist hire” hypothesis needs to include work transferred into appointments;
- full-check revenue is often mislabeled as profit in promotional arithmetic;
- vendor claims and single owner reports are useful leads for verification, not measured general effects;
- one appointment may appear in multiple benefit narratives.

### What the pilot cannot support

- a typical no-show reduction;
- a typical number of hours saved;
- a typical receptionist cost avoided;
- any stated ROI multiple;
- causation by a named product;
- a conclusion that salon software generally pays for itself;
- a claim that VisaxaCRM provides any measured economic benefit.

The Facebook URLs and quoted anecdotes in the supplied report are not used as factual public-article evidence. The five-minute value is retained only as a declared scenario input because the owner explicitly requested that sensitivity test.

## Calculation method

### Cost side

For a declared time horizon:

```text
total incremental cost
= base and threshold subscription charges
+ usage, processing, communication, and marketplace fees
+ required add-ons, integrations, permissions, devices, and support
+ implementation, migration, training, dual-run, and correction labor
+ recurring internal administration
+ allocated transition and exit cost
+ value of work transferred into constrained service capacity
```

### Benefit side

```text
realized benefit
= paid labor cost actually removed
+ a documented hire cost actually avoided
+ incremental contribution profit from additional completed sales
+ measured correction or loss cost actually avoided
```

### Decision result

```text
net economic outcome = realized benefit - total incremental cost
```

The result must retain the period, operating state, alternative, assumptions, and uncertainty. “Negative effective cost” is permissible only as the numerical result of a measured declared scenario, never as a general product attribute.

## Constructed avoided-hire sensitivity

The following values are assumptions, not findings:

| Input | Base case | Verification needed |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Fully loaded receptionist role avoided | $3,600/month | Staffing plan, local wage, expected schedule, taxes/benefits |
| Incremental software and usage | $400/month | Current quote and expected volumes |
| Implementation allocation | $200/month | Contract plus internal time, amortized over declared period |
| Completed appointments | 500/month | Appointment records |
| Work transferred into each appointment | 5 minutes | Observed task time |
| Contribution margin per genuinely displaced bookable hour | $45/hour | Service mix minus variable costs |
| Share of transferred time that displaces profitable bookings | 100% upper bound | Observed capacity and completed-booking evidence |
| Share of the avoided hire attributable to switching software | 100% upper bound | Comparison with current-system and realistic alternative workflows |

```text
500 × 5 minutes = 41.7 theoretical hours transferred
displaced profitable hours = transferred hours × assumed or measured displacement rate
2,500 ÷ 60 × 100% × $45 = $1,875 upper-bound capacity value at risk
$3,600 - $400 - $200 - $1,875 = $1,125 modeled monthly net benefit
```

This is a sensitivity test, not evidence of a positive result. The transferred hours are not automatically displaced profitable hours. At 50% assumed displacement, the modeled capacity cost is $937.50 and the modeled benefit is $2,062.50. At zero displacement, no capacity cost is assigned. If the hire was not documented and genuinely planned, the $3,600 is not an avoided cost. If the current system, a policy change, or another realistic workflow can avoid the same hire at equal or lower cost, the software switch cannot claim the full $3,600. If existing employees absorb the work in paid overtime, that actual cost replaces the assumed capacity value.

## Double-counting control

Maintain two linked ledgers:

1. **Event ledger:** one row per appointment/contact/payment event; record mechanism, outcome, payment, variable cost, and whether another benefit row already uses it.
2. **Time ledger:** one row per task-time change; record role, minutes, paid-hours effect, capacity effect, and chosen financial realization.

Rules:

- one appointment contributes full incremental contribution profit once;
- the same hour cannot be both payroll saving and extra capacity unless separate portions are demonstrated;
- a deposit is not additional revenue when the same completed service is already counted;
- a booking is not recovered revenue until it becomes a completed paid event, unless the analysis explicitly uses expected value and states the probability assumption;
- subscription, processing, add-on, implementation, and transferred-work costs remain separate rows;
- compare against a defined alternative, not against zero.
- attribute an avoided cost to the switch only to the extent that the best realistic non-switch alternative cannot deliver the same outcome at equal or lower cost.

## Claim audit

### SUPPORTED

- Current official pricing uses different threshold units across products.
- Subscription price alone can omit usage, processing, add-on, implementation, internal labor, and exit cost.
- Employer cost can exceed wage because benefits and employment obligations exist.
- Contribution analysis subtracts variable cost rather than treating full revenue as profit.
- Objective ROI claims require evidence appropriate to the claim.

### PARTIALLY SUPPORTED / EDITORIAL SYNTHESIS

- The next operational threshold is a better comparison state than today's invoice. Supported by pricing structures; the decision method is editorial.
- Avoided hiring can be a real benefit. Economically coherent, but no retained salon example proves it here.
- Transferred five-minute tasks can consume service capacity. Arithmetic is valid; prevalence and actual displacement are unknown.
- An event ledger reduces double-counting. This is an editorial control, not a validated accounting standard.

### NOT YET SUPPORTED

- A named salon product reduces no-shows by a specified percentage.
- A named product saves a specified number of staff hours.
- A named product increases retention, bookings, or revenue.
- A salon can remove a receptionist role without reducing service capacity.
- Any vendor pays for itself or has negative effective cost.
- VisaxaCRM produces measurable ROI.

## Publication wording safeguards

- Say “the official page displayed” rather than “the vendor always charges.”
- Date every numeric vendor amount and state region where known.
- Call Muse examples “research leads” or omit them; do not present the Facebook posts as verified cases.
- Call the example “constructed” in its first sentence and beside the result.
- Use “modeled contribution profit,” not “revenue saved,” when variable costs are estimated.
- Use “avoided planned hire,” not “replaced receptionist.”
- Never turn an unknown into zero.

## Remaining uncertainties

1. Exact same-region pricing and taxes for a real buyer.
2. Negotiated, grandfathered, or custom vendor terms.
3. Required add-ons and implementation scope for a declared salon configuration.
4. Real appointment/message/payment volumes.
5. Local fully loaded labor cost and whether a hire is genuinely imminent.
6. Whether transferred work consumes idle time, paid time, or saleable capacity.
7. Service-level variable costs and contribution margin.
8. Attribution of no-show, utilization, retention, and booking changes.
9. Exit/migration costs for the candidate and target system.
