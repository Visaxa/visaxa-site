## Research findings

1. The option set must be derived from the required outcome, not from a favourite remedy.
2. Business as usual belongs in the comparison because accepting the current condition has future costs and risks.
3. An option that cannot meet the required outcome should not win because it is cheap, familiar, or easy.
4. “Supplement” is not inherently smaller once interfaces, duplicate truth, support ownership, training, and ongoing reconciliation are counted.
5. “Replace” is not inherently cleaner once migration, verification, downtime, retraining, continuity, and exit exposure are counted.
6. Past spending is sunk. Only prospective consequences—including still-avoidable contractual costs—belong in the decision.
7. Uncertainty is not a score of zero. Material uncertainty should be named, investigated, or tested through a reversible bounded trial.
8. A trial needs an agreed outcome, evidence, failure condition, owner, and review point; otherwise a workaround can persist without a decision.

## Claim audit

### SUPPORTED

- Structured appraisal starts from objectives and compares plausible options with business as usual (S01).
- Options that fail required objectives should not be treated as value-for-money winners (S01).
- Relevant costs extend beyond purchase price to integration, transition, training, support, and lifecycle burden (S03–S05).
- Technology choices should account for adaptability, reversibility, lock-in, and total cost (S02).
- Prior investment can bias continuation even when future consequences should drive the choice (S01, S06).
- Focused tests can reduce a material uncertainty when decision criteria are defined in advance (S07).
- Published commercial terms are current, scoped evidence and may not match a specific executed agreement (S08–S10).

### PARTIALLY SUPPORTED / EDITORIAL SYNTHESIS

- Compare “recurring operating burden” with “transition burden” as the article’s owner-facing model. The categories are evidence-grounded, but this exact phrasing is editorial synthesis.
- Prefer the viable option with the lowest credible future burden at acceptable risk and reversibility. The rule synthesizes appraisal principles; no source guarantees the result.
- Use a bounded supplement test before replacement in the constructed case. This is illustrative, not a universal sequence.

### NOT YET SUPPORTED

- Any response is generally best for salons.
- Any named vendor will negotiate, repair, export, migrate, integrate, or retain access for a given account.
- Any migration or trial takes a standard number of days.
- A scoring formula can convert all burden and risk into a defensible universal number.
- Visaxa currently solves the scenario.
- Owner discussions establish prevalence or causation. No E16 material is used as product evidence.

## Vendor-terms review

## Evidence gaps and wording controls

- Say “can add” or “may introduce,” never “always costs.”
- Say “possible response,” never “recommended industry practice.”
- Say “constructed case,” never imply a tested salon outcome.
- Say “current official terms for the applicable account must be checked,” never summarize a vendor’s exit promise from a public page alone.
- Do not publish a numeric score, trial duration, ROI, migration estimate, or success rate.
- Do not claim a vendor-specific capability or a Visaxa solution.

## Verdict

The sources support a cautious, owner-facing method for selecting among remedy classes after diagnosis. They do not support a universal answer or a named-product recommendation.
