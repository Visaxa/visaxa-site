# A10 Evidence Pack — Fix the Process or Replace the Software?

## Document control

- Canonical Article ID: A10
- Canonical Decision IDs: D03, D04
- Evidence types in scope: E02, E03, E05, E17
- Access date for all external sources: 2026-07-25
- Status: complete for an evidence-bounded publication candidate; incomplete for any salon-specific or vendor-specific diagnosis
- Scope: evidence only; this document does not change the article graph, decision graph or canonical registries

Local source and claim labels below are retrieval aids inside this pack. They are not new canonical Evidence IDs.

## 1. Evidence verdict

The available evidence supports a cautious diagnostic method:

- observe the real task rather than inferring cause from a complaint or artifact;
- compare observed work with the intended workflow;
- consider task, individual and organizational conditions together;
- use interviews and documentation to explain observations, not replace them;
- test whether clarification, practice and relevant configuration change the result;
- treat a persistent limitation as stronger software evidence only when the required outcome and measurable consequence are explicit.

The evidence does **not** support:

- a diagnosis of any salon or named software product;
- a prevalence claim about workarounds in salons;
- a universal claim that training, process change or configuration will fix a given symptom;
- a universal observation count or test duration;
- a product-replacement recommendation.

## 2. Source records

### SRC-A10-01 — HSE, Introduction to human factors

- Source: *Introduction to human factors*
- Publisher: Health and Safety Executive, United Kingdom
- URL: https://www.hse.gov.uk/humanfactors/introduction.htm
- Publication/update date: no publication or update date displayed on the inspected page
- Access date: 2026-07-25
- Evidence category: E17 — official human-factors guidance
- Supports:
  - the article’s claim that recurring performance problems should not be reduced to either a person or a tool;
  - the distinction among job/task factors, individual factors such as competence, and organizational factors;
  - the caution that interventions considered in isolation can be ineffective.
- Confidence: HIGH for the general multi-factor principle
- Limitations:
  - occupational health and safety guidance, not salon-software research;
  - does not prescribe the A10 five-cause model;
  - does not establish which factor caused a specific operational problem.

### SRC-A10-05 — Boonstra et al., Persisting workarounds in EHR use

- Source: Boonstra A, Jonker TL, van Offenbeek MAG, Vos JFJ. *Persisting workarounds in Electronic Health Record System use: types, risks and benefits*
- Publisher: BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making
- URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12911-021-01548-0
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-021-01548-0
- Publication/update date: published 2021-06-08
- Access date: 2026-07-25
- Evidence category: E17 supported by E02/E03 methods — primary empirical study
- Study basis: single Dutch hospital case; observations of six users and 17 interviews, as reported by the authors
- Supports:
  - workarounds can have different causes, forms, risks and benefits;
  - the visible workaround should not automatically be treated as a uniform error or product verdict;
  - observation and interviews can reveal why work departs from the designed path.
- Confidence: MEDIUM for transfer to a general “symptom is ambiguous” claim; HIGH for what occurred in the studied setting
- Limitations:
  - healthcare information system, not salon CRM;
  - single organization and small observed-user sample;
  - does not establish prevalence or causal effects in service businesses generally.

### SRC-A10-07 — Flanagan et al., Paper and computer workarounds

- Source: Flanagan ME, Saleem JJ, Millitello LG, Russ AL, Doebbeling BN. *Paper- and computer-based workarounds to electronic health record use at three benchmark institutions*
- Publisher: Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association; record hosted by the U.S. National Library of Medicine
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23492593/
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1136/amiajnl-2012-000982
- Publication/update date: online 2013-03-14; journal issue June 2013
- Access date: 2026-07-25
- Evidence category: E02/E03/E17 — primary observational study
- Study basis: 120 staff/providers and 118 patients observed at 11 clinics across three institutions, as reported by the authors
- Supports:
  - superficially similar paper or computer workarounds can have different rationales;
  - workarounds may support efficiency, memory or awareness, while some arise because no correct electronic path is available;
  - the artifact alone is insufficient to choose a remedy.
- Confidence: MEDIUM for the transferable ambiguity principle; HIGH for the observed settings
- Limitations:
  - healthcare and EHR context;
  - study age and institutional setting limit direct transfer;
  - does not show that a side spreadsheet in a salon has any particular cause.

### SRC-A10-08 — Outmazgin, Soffer and Hadar, Workarounds in business processes

- Publication/update date: published 2020

### SRC-A10-09 — Blijleven et al., EHR workaround rationales

## 5. Evidence gaps

### E02 — Direct task observation

Status: **NOT YET AVAILABLE FOR A REAL SALON**

Needed before any case-specific diagnosis:

- task trigger, role, device and context;
- current steps and system state;
- pauses, duplicate writes, handoffs, corrections and exceptions;
- repeated observations before and after intervention;
- measurable consequence such as elapsed time, rework, missed commitment or reconciliation difference.

### E03 — Owner/staff interview

Status: **NOT YET AVAILABLE**

Needed:

- intended rule and perceived exception;
- what each person believes the system requires;
- training/practice history;
- access, time, incentive and responsibility conditions;
- reason for each workaround, checked against observed behavior.

### E05 — Current official product documentation

Status: **CONDITIONAL / NO NAMED PRODUCT**

Needed for a real diagnosis:

- exact product, plan, region and account role;
- current official documentation for relevant settings and permissions;
- captured configuration state and date;
- reproducible result under realistic conditions;
- any documented limitation or support response.

### E17 — Organizational/change research

Status: **SUPPORTED WITH TRANSFERABILITY LIMITS**

Official guidance and primary research support a multi-factor, observational approach. Most empirical studies located are in healthcare information systems. Their contribution is to show plausible ambiguity and method; they cannot establish what happens in salons.

## 6. Required wording constraints

Use:

- “can,” “may,” “in the observed studies,” and “strengthens the case” where transfer or causality is limited;
- “current official documentation” for intended product behavior;
- “realistic task observation” for practical behavior;
- “unresolved” when a required evidence condition is missing;
- “continuing toward replacement evaluation becomes rational” rather than “replace the software.”

Avoid:

- “research proves that most software problems are process problems”;
- “employees resist change” without direct evidence;
- “the system cannot” without exact product/configuration evidence;
- “training solved it” without repeated before/after task evidence;
- “root cause” where only a plausible contributing cause has been identified;
- numerical effectiveness, prevalence or certainty claims.

## 7. Publication safety assessment

### Safe to publish

- the evidence-bounded five-cause distinction;
- the owner-facing observation method;
- the controlled-test sequence as an editorial synthesis;
- the four-condition decision standard, clearly labeled as a decision rule rather than a validated scientific instrument;
- limited references to primary studies as examples of why workaround artifacts are ambiguous;
- explicit evidence gaps and transferability limits.

### Not safe to publish

- any named-vendor finding;
- any diagnosis of a salon;
- any claim that a particular intervention will work;
- any universal count, duration or threshold;
- any prevalence claim about salon workarounds;
- any suggestion that passing A10 proves readiness to buy or migrate.

## 8. Final evidence status

**READY WITH KNOWN LIMITATIONS**

The evidence is sufficient for a cautious early-diagnosis article that teaches no vendor conclusion and makes its synthesis visible. It is not sufficient for product comparison, salon diagnosis, intervention effectiveness or a replacement recommendation.
