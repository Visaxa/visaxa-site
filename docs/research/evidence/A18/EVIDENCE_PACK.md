# A18 Evidence Pack — Evidence Before Trust

Status: **EVIDENCE COMPLETE WITH KNOWN LIMITATIONS**  
Access date for all sources: **October 21, 2026**

## Scope

This pack supports A18 / D15: deciding what evidence is sufficient to rely on a material business-software claim. It does not certify a vendor, validate a salon outcome, or report a Visaxa product test.

## Source records

### A18-S01 — NIST SP 800-53A Rev. 5

- Source: *Assessing Security and Privacy Controls in Information Systems and Organizations*
- Publisher: National Institute of Standards and Technology
- URL: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/a/r5/final
- Publication/update date: January 2022; NIST planning note updated August 27, 2025
- Evidence category: independent authoritative evidence; assessment method
- Supports: evidence can be gathered through distinct methods such as examining artifacts, interviewing people, and testing behavior under specified conditions; assessment scope and rigor should match the objective.
- Confidence: high for the stated NIST method.
- Limitation: security/privacy control assessment guidance, not a salon-software purchasing standard.

### A18-S02 — NCSC Assurance Principles and Claims

- Source: *Software Security Code of Practice — Assurance Principles and Claims (APCs)*
- Publisher: UK National Cyber Security Centre
- URL: https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/software-security-code-of-practice-assurance-principles-claims
- Publication/update date: May 7, 2025; version 1.0
- Evidence category: independent authoritative evidence; claim–argument–evidence method
- Supports: claims can be decomposed into objectively evidentiary subclaims; evidence types vary; incomplete evidence should reveal a gap and next action rather than being treated as complete assurance.
- Confidence: high for the published method.
- Limitation: scoped to software security and resilience; A18 generalizes the reasoning pattern cautiously to operational claims.

### A18-S03 — NCSC guidance for software customers

- Source: *For software customers*
- Publisher: UK National Cyber Security Centre
- URL: https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/section/software-security-code-of-practice/software-customers
- Publication/update date: current page; no separate date displayed in retrieved record
- Evidence category: independent authoritative evidence; buyer assurance
- Supports: buyers can request a vendor assurance document, cross-check claims, seek independent testing when higher assurance is needed, and maintain assurance over time.
- Confidence: high for the NCSC process.
- Limitation: security procurement only; does not establish a universal hierarchy for all operational claims.

### A18-S04 — GOV.UK Digital Marketplace supplier evaluation

- Source: *How Digital Marketplace suppliers have been evaluated*
- Publisher: Government Digital Service / GOV.UK
- URL: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-digital-marketplace-suppliers-have-been-evaluated
- Publication/update date: first published April 18, 2016; page carries later update history
- Evidence category: independent authoritative evidence; requirements-based evaluation
- Supports: supplier inclusion or declaration does not remove the buyer's responsibility to write clear requirements and evaluate whether the service meets them.
- Confidence: high for the stated Digital Marketplace process.
- Limitation: UK public-sector framework guidance, not consumer law or a salon-specific purchasing process.

### A18-S05 — GAO Technology Readiness Assessment Guide

- Source: *Technology Readiness Assessment Guide: Best Practices for Evaluating the Readiness of Technology for Use in Acquisition Programs and Projects*

- URL: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-16-410g
- Publication/update date: August 11, 2016
- Evidence category: independent authoritative evidence; evidence-based decision gates
- Supports: readiness assessment is a systematic, evidence-based process; sufficient evidence and the intended operational environment must be understood before a major commitment.
- Confidence: high for federal acquisition readiness assessment.

### A18-S06 — FTC endorsements and reviews guidance

- Source: *Endorsements, Influencers, and Reviews*
- Publisher: U.S. Federal Trade Commission
- URL: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews
- Publication/update date: current collection; underlying Endorsement Guides revised in 2023
- Evidence category: government guidance; provenance and review limitations
- Supports: endorsements can be misleading, material connections affect credibility, and supposedly independent rankings or reviews may be manipulated.
- Confidence: high for the FTC advertising context.
- Limitation: does not show that any particular review is false and does not make reviews useless; it supports checking provenance and fit.

### A18-S07 — Digital.gov usability testing

- Source: *Usability testing*
- Publisher: U.S. General Services Administration, Digital.gov
- URL: https://digital.gov/guides/research-collaboration/testing/usability
- Publication/update date: page retrieved as current in 2026
- Evidence category: government practice guidance; observed task testing
- Supports: observing users attempting defined tasks reveals issues that descriptions alone cannot establish; a script and recorded observations improve the usefulness of the test.
- Confidence: high for the stated usability-testing practice.
- Limitation: a usability test is not a reliability, security, migration, or legal assessment.

## Evidence boundaries for public prose

- Official documentation supports only what is documented for its stated product, plan, region, date, role, configuration, and conditions.
- A sample output shows the inspected artifact, not every export or every account.
- A demonstration shows observed behavior under the demonstrated conditions, not durable production reliability.
- Contract language supports the written obligation or limitation, not operational performance.
- Independent assurance supports only its declared scope.
- Missing evidence remains **unknown**; it does not prove absence or deception.
- The salon scenario in A18 is constructed. No vendor, salon owner, or staff member was tested or interviewed.

## Publication verdict

The core method is publishable as an evidence-bounded editorial synthesis. Named-vendor performance claims and claims that the four-verdict record is validated remain blocked.
