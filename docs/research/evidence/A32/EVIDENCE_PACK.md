# A32 Evidence Pack — When Is It Safe to Shut Down the Old System?

Access date: **2026-10-10**

Status: **COMPLETE FOR PUBLICATION WITH KNOWN LIMITATIONS**

## Evidence boundary

No salon account, migration, cancellation, archive, dispute, or rollback was tested. The salon scenario is explicitly constructed. Public documentation describes only its stated product, jurisdiction, and date; it cannot prove the terms or account state of a particular salon. This pack supports a closure method, not legal, tax, accounting, or vendor-specific advice.

## Research findings

1. Deactivation can be materially different from merely ceasing daily use; official product documentation may state lost access or irreversibility.
2. A successful migration does not settle whether evidence, open obligations, or records remain uniquely dependent on the old account.
3. “Downloaded” and “archived” are not equivalent. A defensible archive is retrievable, interpretable, attributable, and retained under an owned policy.
4. Open obligations do not all need to be zero, but each needs an owner, evidence location, and next action outside the old system.
5. Final reconciliation needs a defined cutover boundary. The article's “closing line” is an editorial synthesis, not a prescribed accounting standard.
6. A rollback window should close against agreed evidence and the last reversible point, not a universal number of days.
7. Access and integration closure follows evidence capture; account cancellation is last.

## Claim audit

### SUPPORTED

- Some official deactivation instructions direct users to export records first and state that account access may become unavailable or irreversible (S01).
- Disputes can require evidence, deadlines, permissions, and downloadable reports (S02).
- Official exports may be report-specific, filtered, and permission-dependent (S03).
- Some destructive actions require operational items such as future appointments or unpaid invoices to be resolved first (S04).
- Electronic records used for US federal-tax purposes require preservation, retrieval, reproduction, and integrity controls within the source's scope (S05–S07).
- Access inventories and timely privilege termination are recognized security controls (S08).
- Return, deletion, backups, and retention can be distinct contractual/privacy conditions (S09–S10).

### PARTIALLY SUPPORTED / EDITORIAL SYNTHESIS

- “Nothing material exists only here” is the article's owner-facing closure rule.
- The seven-part closure packet combines supported evidence categories but is not an official universal standard.
- Open obligations may remain if ownership and evidence are safely transferred; this is a risk-control judgment, not a guarantee.
- The cancellation order is a cautious synthesis and must yield to the salon's actual contract, jurisdiction, and technical dependencies.

### NOT YET SUPPORTED

- A universal number of parallel-run days.
- A universal legal or tax retention period for all salon records.
- Any named vendor provides a complete, relationship-preserving archive.
- Any export can be re-imported or understood without testing.
- Reactivation is possible unless current official evidence for that product and account proves it.
- Visaxa currently supplies the described closure evidence.

## Wording controls

- Say “can” or “may,” never imply every vendor removes access in the same way.
- Say “check the current documentation and your executed agreement,” not “the vendor must.”
- Call the example constructed; do not imply an observed salon migration.
- Do not prescribe a retention period or present legal/tax guidance as universal.
- Do not claim a complete archive from the presence of an export button.
- Do not claim that elapsed time proves rollback safety.
- Do not claim a Visaxa capability.

## Verdict

The evidence supports a cautious, owner-facing shutdown gate. It does not support a universal timeline, legal conclusion, complete-vendor comparison, or product claim.
