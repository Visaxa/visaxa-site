## 5. Final official-source check

Access date: **2026-11-12**, America/New_York.

### US pricing

Fresha's official US-rendered pricing result currently displays:

- `20% one-time commission`;
- `Minimum fee of $6 per client applies`;
- no fee for returning marketplace clients.

The generic pricing URL is region-sensitive and can return other currencies or even a different marketplace-fee treatment. Public copy therefore says **“US pricing page”**, uses `$`, and requires a US-region recheck on the actual publication date.

### Help Center rules confirmed

The current official Help Center confirms:

- the client must be absent from the existing client list at booking;
- the client first discovers the business through the Fresha marketplace and then books online;
- the client confirms no prior visit;
- manually added and previously imported clients are excluded;
- the fee is one-time and calculated from the first completed appointment;
- checkout additions, online memberships/gift cards/products, tips and service charges are excluded from the fee base;
- a maximum cap exists, but its amount is not published;
- a client-side online cancellation defers the fee to the next booking;
- a staff-side cancellation, reschedule or unmanaged appointment does not avoid it;
- a same-day no-show marking defers it;
- the fee is non-refundable;
- a prior marketplace view can retain attribution when the later booking occurs through the salon's website or social page;
- unlisting the marketplace profile disables Fresha online booking channels.

### Pricing-summary discrepancy

The pricing page summarizes acquisition by channel. The Help Center adds the earlier-discovery condition. The candidate does not declare either page wrong. It states that the pricing page is a summary and the Help Center contains the operational rule. A disputed charge still requires the account's client-source history.

### Unknown maximum

Fresha says a maximum cap exists but does not publish the amount on the checked pages. The candidate does not invent a cap and removes the risky `$500 = $100` example.
