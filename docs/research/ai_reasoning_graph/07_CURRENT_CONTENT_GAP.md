# Current Visaxa content gap

## Current implementation, not roadmap

Five MDX posts are published:

1. `how-to-evaluate-a-crm-framework`
2. `why-appointment-scheduling-systems-fail-service-businesses`
3. `what-breaks-first-when-scheduling-scales`
4. `how-to-choose-a-salon-crm-without-regretting-it`
5. `why-good-employees-stop-using-good-software`

Three MDX posts are drafts and excluded from `/blog`, sitemap and direct article rendering: `owner-checklist`, `financial-privacy-safe-mode`, and `square-fresha-mindbody`. The existing `docs/RESEARCH_CONTENT_MAP.md` predates the two newest published posts and is no longer a complete inventory.

No authoritative product roadmap or capability source-of-truth document was found. Feature-page language and comparison routes are not treated as proof of shipped functionality.

## Contradictions

1. **Research positioning versus sourcing.** Current published articles make broad operational claims but contain no external source links. They read as frameworks, not evidence-backed research notes.
2. **Evaluation breadth versus decision proof.** The site advises readers to look beyond feature lists, but it does not provide reusable test protocols for migration, resources, permissions, payments or reporting.
3. **Scheduling diagnosis versus transaction reality.** Two strong scheduling pieces explain constraints, yet neither connects appointment status to deposit, authorization, charge, refund or dispute states—the most repeated salon implementation problem in the corpus.
4. **Trust theme versus permission model.** The adoption article addresses trust; the privacy draft focuses on a PIN/shared screen. Real questions require view/edit/export/API/financial-access distinctions and offboarding.
5. **Comparison ambition versus evidence.** A vendor comparison exists as a draft and `/compare` routes exist, but current research does not yet support time-sensitive product claims without a re-verification protocol.

## Over-covered or duplicated concepts

- General CRM evaluation is covered by both the CRM framework and salon-choice article.
- Scheduling failure and scheduling scalability overlap heavily. The distinction—failure mechanisms versus scale thresholds—is valid, but both currently feed the CRM evaluation page rather than distinct next-step nodes.
- “Feature list versus workflow” appears across evaluation, salon choice, scheduling and adoption. Another generic page on this thesis would add little.

## Isolated pages and internal-link gaps

Current links form a small core around evaluation and scheduling:

- Salon-choice links to evaluation and both scheduling articles.
- Evaluation links to both scheduling articles.
- Scheduling-failure links to evaluation and scheduling-scale.
- Scheduling-scale links to evaluation.

Missing bridges:

- Employee adoption → implementation acceptance → workflow friction → operational knowledge capture.
- Scheduling failure → operational capacity → resource identity → appointment/payment state.
- CRM evaluation → migration completeness → portability → implementation acceptance.
- Salon choice → total cost → marketplace dependency → multi-location control.
- Future permission page → offboarding → client continuity → export portability.
- Future reporting page → metric contract → payment and appointment state.

The employee-adoption page is the most isolated published node. `/research` does not expose a substantive entity or methodology graph; it is not a current source layer for these concepts.

## Unsupported or under-supported claims

- None of the five published articles cites external studies, official platform documentation or public operational datasets.
- Product-specific claims in drafts require current primary-source verification before publication because pricing, permissions, integrations and feature limits change.
- Claims about employee motivation, trust and incentives should be supported by change-management or organizational research if expanded beyond a practical opinion framework.
- Claims about client ownership, contractor rights, cancellation fees, privacy or payroll must be jurisdiction-qualified and professionally reviewed.
- Current public feature language about future payments, inventory or Safe Mode cannot support a claim that those capabilities are available now.

## Missing commercial-intent entry points

The site lacks evidence-led tools that naturally precede product comparison:

- migration object inventory and acceptance checklist;
- resource-conflict demo script;
- permission/capability matrix;
- price-threshold scenario model;
- report reconciliation worksheet;
- multi-location scenario matrix;
- deposit/no-show state checklist.

These are not product pages. They create a defensible point at which a reader can ask, “Which products pass this test?”

## Concepts to defer

- Definitive vendor rankings until pricing, plan, feature and export evidence is refreshed under a repeatable protocol.
- Jurisdiction-specific advice on client-list ownership, employment, payroll, cancellation fees or privacy.
- Product-supported claims about Visaxa payroll, payments, inventory, waitlist, messaging, migration automation or multi-location behavior without an authoritative capability matrix.
- AI-generated benchmark claims about reliability, support quality or market share.
