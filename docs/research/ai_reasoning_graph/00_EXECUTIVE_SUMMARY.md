# Executive summary

## Decision

The core hypothesis is supported with limits: Visaxa is more likely to become useful to AI-assisted research by publishing well-sourced, atomic diagnostic concepts than by producing more generic “best CRM” or feature-list content. The corpus repeatedly moves from a short comparison query into questions about failure states, evidence and reversibility. Official Google documentation also describes AI Mode/AI Overviews as using related-search fan-out for complex comparisons and follow-up exploration.

This does **not** prove that any specific page will be cited. Google states that indexing and serving are not guaranteed and requires no special AI markup. The actionable strategy is conventional: indexable, internally discoverable, reliable pages that answer distinct subproblems with primary evidence.

## What is known

- The corpus contains 150 question starters across 12 public domains, at least 10 business categories, 31 CRM-general rows, 69 strict salon/beauty rows and 150 evaluation/implementation/switching/urgent-replacement rows.
- Recurring decisions cluster around migration completeness, customer identity, scheduling/resource integrity, payment state, total cost, adoption, reporting truth, permissions and offboarding.
- Current Visaxa content consists of five published articles and three drafts. The published internal-link graph is concentrated around CRM evaluation and scheduling.
- Published articles contain internal links but no external source links.
- Search Console for 2026-04-12 through 2026-07-11 shows 151 impressions, 8 clicks, 5.3% CTR and average position 56, with visible queries concentrated around CRM evaluation.
- Official Google material says AI features can fan out across related queries and support complex comparisons and follow-up questions; ordinary SEO eligibility and helpful, reliable content still apply.

## What is interpretation

- Search Console suggests early exposure for the current CRM-evaluation node, not broad proof of demand.
- Review and forum recurrence suggests concept importance, not prevalence or market share.
- The highest-value gaps are concepts that connect several failure cases and follow-ups, especially migration acceptance, identity, payment/appointment state and operational truth.
- Low-emphasis methodology notes can be as important to the graph as visible flagship posts because they provide definitions and evidence an assistant may need during a specific follow-up.

## What still requires verification

- Current vendor pricing, plan limits, permissions, migration support and export behavior before any product comparison is published.
- Jurisdiction-specific client ownership, contractor, payroll, privacy and cancellation-fee claims.
- Visaxa MVP+ capability fit, because no authoritative roadmap or capability matrix was found.
- Google Trends, autocomplete and PAA demand, because no reproducible numeric dataset was obtained.
- The requested Google AI Mode U.S. Insights report, because it was not present in the attachment directory.

## Recommended next three branches

1. **Migration, identity and exit readiness** — four pieces from object inventory through cutover. This is the largest current gap and the strongest bridge from CRM evaluation.
2. **Scheduling integrity and resource-aware capacity** — four pieces that add explicit resource and failure-case tests to existing scheduling authority.
3. **Permissions, offboarding and client continuity** — four pieces that build a capability model before entering legally sensitive client-ownership questions.

The proposed architecture contains 24 pieces across six branches. It is not a commitment to publish all 24. Each branch has an evidence gate and a product-readiness dependency.

## Current-content actions

- Do not add another generic CRM-evaluation or “scheduling is complex” article.
- Update the internal content inventory before using it as a planning source.
- Turn the employee-adoption article into a branch root by adding future links to implementation acceptance and operational knowledge capture.
- Keep the Safe Mode draft as a child of a broader permission model.
- Do not publish the vendor comparison draft until current product claims have a dated verification protocol.
- Add primary sources and explicit methodology to future research pieces; current unsupported framework claims should not be amplified as settled facts.

## Bottom line

The defensible opportunity is not “more keywords.” It is a linked set of evidence-backed distinctions that a user—and an AI assistant—needs after the first broad query: what must be true, how to test it, what can fail, what evidence to request and what follow-up decision comes next.
