# AI reasoning paths

These are analytical reconstructions of the information path a high-quality assistant should follow. They are not claims about private chain of thought or proprietary ranking logic.

## Path 1 — “Which salon CRM should I choose?”

**A. User wording**

“Which salon CRM is best for a five-person salon that may open a second location?”

**B. Likely underlying decision, fear, or desired outcome**

Select a system without paying for unused enterprise complexity or needing a disruptive second migration. Secondary fears: client-booking friction, hidden fees, staff rejection and loss of data control.

**C. Clarifying dimensions the AI should consider**

Business model; staff versus contractors; services, rooms and equipment; deposits; retail; payroll/commission; client accounts; current data; second-location horizon; budget; jurisdiction; must-have integrations.

**D. Diagnostic concepts required**

System boundary, workflow fit, operational capacity, total cost, location hierarchy, permissions, portability, implementation acceptance.

**E. Evidence types the AI should seek**

Official feature/pricing/export documentation; current product limitations; implementation scope; firsthand owner evidence; contract terms; status/release history; role and resource models.

**F. Likely search-query fan-out**

`salon software staff room resource scheduling`; `vendor multi-location permissions`; `vendor client export appointments notes`; `vendor deposit checkout reconciliation`; `vendor pricing per staff location`; `vendor cancellation contract implementation`.

**G. Existing Visaxa content that could support the answer**

`how-to-choose-a-salon-crm-without-regretting-it`; `how-to-evaluate-a-crm-framework`; both scheduling articles.

**H. Missing Visaxa concept nodes**

Right-sized system boundary; migration completeness; capability-level permissions; total-cost thresholds; multi-location control plane; implementation acceptance.

**I. Natural follow-up questions likely to emerge**

“How do I test resource conflicts in a demo?” “What data must be exportable?” “Which plan supports two locations?” “How much training is normal?”

**J. Commercial transition point**

After the owner has a workflow/failure-case scorecard and asks which products can demonstrate those conditions with current documentation.

## Path 2 — “Is there a free CRM or booking tool?”

**A. User wording**

“What free booking software should a solo barber use?”

**B. Likely underlying decision, fear, or desired outcome**

Avoid fixed commitment before demand is proven. Secondary fears: transaction fees, losing clients to a marketplace, paying at the first hire and being unable to export later.

**C. Clarifying dimensions the AI should consider**

Appointment volume/value; payment mix; staff-growth horizon; SMS usage; marketplace acquisition; client-login requirement; export coverage; support; willingness to split tools.

**D. Diagnostic concepts required**

Free plan versus trial, upgrade cliff, transaction economics, marketplace dependency, switching cost, client-data portability.

**E. Evidence types the AI should seek**

Current pricing schedules; plan limits; payment/message fees; marketplace terms; export documentation; cancellation terms; verified owner experiences.

**F. Likely search-query fan-out**

`vendor free plan staff limits`; `vendor payment SMS fees`; `vendor marketplace new client fee`; `vendor export clients appointments`; `vendor pricing second employee`; `free booking software data ownership`.

**G. Existing Visaxa content that could support the answer**

CRM evaluation and salon selection articles discuss fit but do not model price thresholds.

**H. Missing Visaxa concept nodes**

Free is a pricing model; workflow-adjusted total cost; growth threshold; exit cost; marketplace versus direct relationship.

**I. Natural follow-up questions likely to emerge**

“At what monthly volume is paid cheaper?” “Can I take my appointment history?” “Will clients need an app?” “What happens when I hire?”

**J. Commercial transition point**

When the user supplies volume, payment and growth assumptions and asks for product-level cost scenarios.

## Path 3 — “Why does my team hate our CRM?”

**A. User wording**

“The software is good; why do employees keep using spreadsheets and texting?”

**B. Likely underlying decision, fear, or desired outcome**

Recover adoption without replacing the system or losing strong employees. Secondary fears: surveillance, role loss, slower service and exposing management mistakes.

**C. Clarifying dimensions the AI should consider**

Which tasks are bypassed; duplicate entry; mobile conditions; incentives; permissions; exceptions; who designed the workflow; whether workarounds carry needed data; management use of metrics.

**D. Diagnostic concepts required**

Workflow friction, shadow systems, task-time measurement, role identity, incentive alignment, operational knowledge capture, change acceptance.

**E. Evidence types the AI should seek**

Task observation; system audit logs; staff interviews; change-management research; role/permission design; error and rework metrics.

**F. Likely search-query fan-out**

`CRM user adoption duplicate entry research`; `frontline staff workflow change involvement`; `employee software surveillance trust`; `shadow spreadsheets operational workaround`; `CRM task completion audit`.

**G. Existing Visaxa content that could support the answer**

`why-good-employees-stop-using-good-software` is a strong entry node.

**H. Missing Visaxa concept nodes**

Workaround classification; operational knowledge capture; adoption acceptance metrics; incentive-system interaction; role transition.

**I. Natural follow-up questions likely to emerge**

“Which workarounds should we keep?” “How do we measure friction?” “What permissions reduce fear?” “When is the software actually the wrong fit?”

**J. Commercial transition point**

After diagnosis shows structural workflow mismatch and the user asks which systems support the observed exception paths.

## Path 4 — “How do I migrate without losing data?”

**A. User wording**

“We imported our clients; how do we know the migration worked?”

**B. Likely underlying decision, fear, or desired outcome**

Go live without losing clients, notes, consent, future appointments or cash flow. Secondary fear: duplicates and discovering failure after the old account is canceled.

**C. Clarifying dimensions the AI should consider**

Source/target schemas; canonical identity; related records; attachments; consent; open balances; recurring appointments; payment tokens; integrations; downtime; retention obligations; rollback window.

**D. Diagnostic concepts required**

Migration completeness, identity resolution, schema mapping, dry run, reconciliation, cutover, rollback, operational acceptance.

**E. Evidence types the AI should seek**

Official import/export schemas; field limitations; migration-service scope; API docs; error files; retention terms; owner reports; sample reconciliation.

**F. Likely search-query fan-out**

`source export clients notes appointments`; `target import supported fields`; `CRM merge behavior history consent`; `booking platform recurring appointment migration`; `payment token portability`; `migration rollback checklist`.

**G. Existing Visaxa content that could support the answer**

CRM evaluation mentions portability; draft owner launch checklist is adjacent but not sufficient.

**H. Missing Visaxa concept nodes**

Migration acceptance contract; identity resolution before mapping; relationship/attachment reconciliation; dual-run/cutover; rollback readiness.

**I. Natural follow-up questions likely to emerge**

“What cannot be migrated?” “How large should the dry run be?” “How do we detect duplicates?” “When can we cancel the old system?”

**J. Commercial transition point**

When the migration inventory is complete and the user asks which products or partners support the required object types and rollback evidence.

**G. Existing Visaxa content that could support the answer**

**I. Natural follow-up questions likely to emerge**

**J. Commercial transition point**

After the business has explicit constraint scenarios and asks which products can pass them live.

## Path 6 — “Can it take deposits and handle no-shows?”

**A. User wording**

“Can this system take a 30% deposit and apply it at checkout?”

**B. Likely underlying decision, fear, or desired outcome**

Make cancellation policy enforceable without confusing clients or staff. Secondary fears: uncollectible fees, duplicate charges and accounting mismatch.

**C. Clarifying dimensions the AI should consider**

Deposit versus prepayment versus authorization; fixed/percentage; cancellation window; refundability; card-on-file; taxes; tips; split tenders; disputes; local law; appointment-status corrections.

**D. Diagnostic concepts required**

Payment-state machine, appointment-state machine, ledger linkage, reconciliation, exception correction, policy communication.

**E. Evidence types the AI should seek**

Official payment docs; processor terms; refund/dispute rules; checkout documentation; jurisdiction-specific professional advice where needed; owner failure cases.

**F. Likely search-query fan-out**

`vendor deposit versus prepayment`; `vendor authorization hold expiration`; `apply deposit at checkout`; `no show fee card on file`; `deposit refund dispute terms`; `appointment status payment audit`.

**G. Existing Visaxa content that could support the answer**

Scheduling articles mention operational failure but do not explain payment states.

**H. Missing Visaxa concept nodes**

Appointment-payment state separation; deposit reconciliation; status correction; cancellation policy enforceability.

**I. Natural follow-up questions likely to emerge**

“What if staff marks no-show by mistake?” “How are tips and taxes calculated?” “Can the client dispute it?” “What appears in owner reports?”

**J. Commercial transition point**

When policy and state requirements are explicit and the owner asks which products implement them without manual ledger work.

## Path 7 — “Can employees see clients but not take the list?”

**A. User wording**

**C. Clarifying dimensions the AI should consider**

**D. Diagnostic concepts required**

**E. Evidence types the AI should seek**

Official permission matrices; API/export controls; audit documentation; security principles; privacy obligations; firsthand admin cases.

**F. Likely search-query fan-out**

`vendor permission export contacts`; `vendor field level financial visibility`; `vendor audit export API`; `salon staff client notes permissions`; `CRM offboarding checklist`; `least privilege service business`.

**G. Existing Visaxa content that could support the answer**

**H. Missing Visaxa concept nodes**

**I. Natural follow-up questions likely to emerge**

“Can managers export only their location?” “What does the API expose?” “How fast can access be revoked?” “Who can see payroll and revenue?”

**J. Commercial transition point**

After a role-capability matrix exists and the user asks which products can implement it without giving global admin access.

**G. Existing Visaxa content that could support the answer**

**G. Existing Visaxa content that could support the answer**

**H. Missing Visaxa concept nodes**
