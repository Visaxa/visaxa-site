## Research result: five classifications after the prerequisite gate

The broad workflow/configuration/software taxonomy belongs to A10. Once those basic exclusions and A29 reconciliation have occurred, A30 owns these narrower classifications:

1. **Local-condition mismatch.** Data, role/permission, device, location, timing, integration, or another recorded condition explains why apparently identical events differ.
2. **Entitlement boundary.** The capability exists but is unavailable under the account's current documented plan, region, account type, add-on, or commercial conditions.
3. **Designed-fit boundary.** The product behaves as documented but that behavior cannot meet the salon's required operating model.
4. **Possible defect or incident.** Documented prerequisites appear met and a repeatable result still differs from documented behavior. This supports escalation; it does not prove internal root cause.
5. **Unknown.** Available evidence cannot distinguish the remaining explanations without inventing certainty.

Data quality, permissions, and integrations are not separate top-level conclusions. They are common local conditions whose evidence can route the diagnosis.

## Classification sequence

1. Freeze one case: event, expected outcome, observed outcome, timestamp, consequence, and scope.
2. Confirm the A10 prerequisite gate: agreed rule, ordinary execution/training, basic configuration, and basic access have already been checked.
3. Capture differentiating local conditions: relevant data, role/permission, location/device, integration, timing, plan, region, and documentation version.
4. Separate unavailable capability from documented-but-unsuitable behavior.
5. Where safe, repeat a privacy-safe equivalent while holding recorded conditions steady or changing one plausible condition.
6. Classify the evidence; preserve unknown rather than infer internal cause.
7. Package the evidence for support or A31 and stop before stay/supplement/renegotiate/switch economics.

For urgent harm, contain the effect first; containment is not diagnosis.

## Central thesis

**A verified discrepancy proves an exception, not a defect: the owner must preserve its scope, compare local and commercial conditions with current documented behavior, and classify only what the evidence supports.**
