I thought the booking link would answer the question.

If a new client booked through Fresha's marketplace, the marketplace fee would apply. If the client booked through our own website or social page, it would not.

Then I read Fresha's detailed help page rather than stopping at the pricing summary.

The documented rule followed where the client first discovered the business—not only the link used for the final booking. A new client could view the Fresha marketplace profile, leave, and later book through the salon's website or social page. Fresha says that sequence can still qualify for its marketplace new-client fee.

That changed the question from “Which link did the client click?” to “What client journey did Fresha record?”

The detailed Help Center says the client must be new to the business, first discover it through the Fresha marketplace, and then make an online booking. A prior marketplace view can matter even when the eventual booking is through the salon's website or social media. ## The pricing summary left out the order of events

Fresha's pricing FAQ says a salon is not charged the marketplace fee for new clients who find it through its own website, Instagram, or Facebook. It also says a one-time fee applies when a new client discovers the business through the Fresha marketplace.

That summary sounds like a clean channel split.

The detailed Help Center adds a condition that the summary does not spell out. Fresha says the fee includes a new client who views the marketplace profile first and then books through another online channel, including the salon's website or social pages.

The two documents are easier to reconcile as a summary and an operational rule, but the difference should not be hidden. The safe reading is:

- **Own website or social page, with no earlier marketplace discovery:** the marketplace new-client fee does not apply on that basis.
- **Marketplace profile first, then the salon's website or social page:** the fee can apply if the other new-client conditions are also met.
- **Client already exists on the salon's Fresha client list:** the marketplace new-client fee does not apply.

For a disputed charge, the booking link alone is incomplete evidence. Fresha points owners to the client-source history, which records discovery, the first online booking, prior-visit confirmation, appointment completion, and the fee when charged.

## “New” means more than a first online booking

I next assumed that every first online booking counted as a new marketplace client.

Fresha's verification conditions are narrower. According to the checked Help Center, the fee applies when all three conditions line up:

- the client is not already on the business's client list at the time of booking;
- the client first discovers the business on the Fresha marketplace and then makes an online booking;
- while booking, the client confirms they have not visited the business before.

The same page says the fee does not apply when the client was already on the list, was added manually, was imported before the first booking, or confirms a prior visit.

That made the client list part of the fee calculation. Importing existing clients before listing the business on the marketplace was no longer just a migration task. It was a way to reduce the chance that a regular client would be classified as new. Fresha also recommends keeping the list organized and merging duplicate profiles.

Those steps reduce classification risk; they do not prove that any specific disputed charge is wrong. A real dispute still depends on the account's client-source record and the applicable Fresha terms.

## The 20% does not use every number at checkout

The US-rendered pricing page showed a 20% one-time commission and a $6 minimum per marketplace new client when checked on November 12, 2026.

The detailed Help Center says the calculation starts with the value of the client's first completed appointment as booked online. It excludes:

- services or products the team adds later during checkout;
- memberships, gift cards, or products bought online;
- tips or service charges added during checkout.

Discount timing matters too. If the client books a predefined online discount, Fresha says the discounted booked price is used. If the team applies a discount later at checkout, Fresha says the fee still uses the value originally booked.

Changing the appointment price afterward does not change that documented base.

For values below any applicable cap, the arithmetic is:

**Fee before any applicable cap:** the greater of 20% of the qualifying original booked value or $6.

- **$20 qualifying original booked value:** $6 minimum.
- **$30 qualifying original booked value:** $6.
- **$50 qualifying original booked value:** $10.
- **$100 qualifying original booked value:** $20.

These are constructed arithmetic examples, not invoice predictions. Fresha says a maximum cap applies to higher-value services, but neither the checked US pricing page nor the detailed Help Center published the cap amount. A high-value booking therefore cannot be estimated safely from an uncapped 20% calculation alone.

## A cancellation did not always mean the same thing

I then treated “cancelled” as one state.

Fresha's documentation separates who takes the action and how the appointment is recorded:

- **Client cancels online through their Fresha account:** the fee is deferred and uses the value of the next appointment booked.
- **The salon or a team member cancels for the client:** Fresha says the fee still applies.
- **The appointment is rescheduled or left unmanaged:** Fresha says the fee still applies.
- **The client is marked as a no-show on the same day:** Fresha says the fee is deferred; if the client books again, it uses the next appointment.
- **The appointment is not checked out:** Fresha says the fee still applies and recommends managing appointments on the day they occur.

Among the cases described on the checked help page, a client-side online cancellation and a same-day no-show marking defer the charge. They do not create a refund for a fee already charged.

## The fee is documented as non-refundable

Fresha's Help Center describes the marketplace new-client fee as non-refundable.

That makes prevention and record review more useful than assuming a later appointment outcome will reverse the charge. Before marketplace listing, Fresha recommends importing existing clients. In day-to-day use, its documentation points to current client records, same-day appointment management, and client-source history.

I also wondered whether a salon could keep Fresha-powered website and social booking while dropping marketplace exposure. Fresha's current opt-out answer says no: its profile must remain listed on the marketplace to take those online bookings, and unlisting disables all Fresha online booking channels.

That is a documented product rule, not a judgment about whether the trade-off is worthwhile for a particular salon.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Fresha marketplace new-client fee charged only once?

Fresha's checked pricing and Help Center pages describe it as a one-time fee for each qualifying new client. Returning appointments by that client do not incur the fee again.

### Why could a website or Instagram booking still be charged?

Fresha's detailed Help Center says a new client who viewed the marketplace profile first can still qualify when they later book through another online channel. Check the account's client-source history rather than relying only on the final booking link.

### Does a checkout discount reduce the fee?

According to Fresha's checked Help Center, no. A predefined discount selected in the original online booking changes the booked value, but a discount applied later at checkout does not change the fee base.

### What happens when the client cancels?

Fresha says an online cancellation made by the client through their account defers the fee to the next booking. A cancellation made by the salon on the client's behalf does not have the same documented result.

### Is there a maximum fee?

Fresha says a maximum cap applies to higher-value services, but the checked public pages do not publish its amount. A specific numeric cap should not be quoted without current official evidence.

### Can a salon unlist from the marketplace and keep Fresha online booking links?

Fresha's checked Help Center says unlisting the marketplace profile disables all Fresha online booking channels.
